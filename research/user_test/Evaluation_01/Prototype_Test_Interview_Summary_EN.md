# DECO3500 Initial Prototype Test: Combined Interview Summary

**Prototype:** Blue study Token and Tower  
**Participants:** P01, P02 and P03  
**Test setup:** Two study sessions per participant; 8 seconds simulated one hour. The study Token was the only activity token tested.


### Evidence used

This summary combines the marked observation sheets in `P01.pdf`, `P02.pdf` and `P03(1).pdf` with the corresponding P01–P03 interview transcripts whose Chinese wording was revised after transcription. The interview sections on pages 3–4 of the PDF forms are largely blank; interview findings below come from the revised transcripts, while the counts come from the marked observation sheets. The three sessions were short formative tests, so the findings describe participants' immediate understanding and expectations, not a sustained change in gaming behaviour.

Across the three marked sheets, **3/3** completed the second flip independently, **2/3** had a clear Token rating, and **2/3** were marked as answering the one-hour/two-hour question correctly. The sheets marked Tower meaning clear for **3/3**, but the interviews reveal uncertainty about the exact duration and what stacking communicates. These ratings should therefore be reported alongside the spoken explanations, not treated as stand-alone proof of comprehension.

### Main findings

1. **The basic Token-to-Tower relationship was recognizable, but the interaction was not consistently reliable.** Participants associated the Token with progress and the Tower with a record of completed activity. P01 called the Token unresponsive; P02's first 180-degree flip did not bring up the expected progress; P03 also encountered a failed attempt. An unreliable response makes it difficult to distinguish an incorrect gesture from an unclear interface.

2. **A growing pattern did not always communicate a precise duration.** P02 understood that blocks accumulate yet was marked incorrect on the one-hour/two-hour check. P03 understood one block as one hour after the facilitator explained it, while saying that the meaning of stacking was unclear. The Tower should make the unit, activity type and cumulative total legible without relying on a verbal explanation.

3. **The display needs more distinct states.** Participants described dots, a progress bar and a falling shape, but P03 was uncertain about the initial icon and suggested an area that fills or a rotating movement instead of one horizontal line. P02 wanted explicit time information; P01 wanted clearer physical response. The start, active timing and completed states need to be distinguishable at a glance.

4. **Shared display changes what participants are willing to record.** P01 said they might show study and conceal gaming or pretend to study. P02 saw possible accountability in a shared setting but also anticipated comparison pressure and wanted separate columns for different users. P03 was less interested in roommates' data, expressed some privacy concern, and said comparison would depend on whether the people shared a similar study context. These were responses to a **hypothetical** multi-user scenario; the current hardware did not demonstrate it.

5. **Hourly interaction could prevent regular use.** P03 expected it would be impractical to return and flip the Token every hour. P02 also questioned how the system would represent different task durations. P03 thought that seeing a long gaming session might raise awareness, but would not necessarily make them stop playing. The current evidence supports a reflection tool more strongly than a claim about reducing gaming time.

### Priorities for the next iteration

| Priority | Change to explore | What to check in a follow-up test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Make a valid flip register consistently and provide immediate acknowledgement. | Can a new user tell whether timing has started without a facilitator? Note failed and repeated flips. |
| 2 | Clarify the waiting, timing and completion displays on the Token. | Can users describe each state and tell when the session ends? |
| 3 | Show that each Tower unit represents one hour and make the cumulative total explicit. | After one and two simulated hours, can users state the total and explain the stack without a hint? |
| 4 | Explore a start/end interaction that can record sessions longer than one hour. | Can users represent a multi-hour study or gaming session without returning every hour? |
| 5 | Prototype private/shared modes and visibly separated user columns before evaluating social effects. | Which activities would users actually choose to share, and how do they identify whose record is whose? |

**Limits:** Three participants, one blue study Token, two compressed sessions and no longitudinal use. The social questions concerned an imagined shared Tower. The observation sheets leave most free-text and interview fields empty, so the revised transcripts supply the detail behind the themes. No SUS score or evidence of clinical effectiveness was collected.
