# Gaming Experience Interview — Interviewee

## Interview Questions

**1. Do you play video games regularly? What games do you usually play? Which one have you been playing the most recently?**

I usually play League of Legends, CS:GO, Overwatch, and Teamfight Tactics — mostly online multiplayer games. That said, I also play a lot of single-player games; it's roughly a 50/50 split between single-player and online games in terms of time. The one I've been playing most recently is League of Legends.

**2. What do you like most about this game? Why is this part important or appealing to you?**

Being able to play with girls — that's the biggest draw for me. If I'm playing alone, I basically won't play; I rarely play by myself.

**3. Can you walk me through your most recent experience playing this game, from when you decided to start playing to when you stopped?**

The most recent time was simple — they called me to play, so I started, and when they were done and logged off, I stopped too. Basically I just follow along with the group. The group size varies — sometimes it's just two of us, sometimes more — but there's always at least one girl in the group; I won't play in an all-guys group. So for me, this game is more of a social thing — there's a bit of a "meeting people" element to it.

**4. Can you think of a recent time when you planned to stop but ended up playing much longer than intended? What happened?**

Yes. Last semester, two days before finals, my procrastination kicked in — after I finished what I needed to do, I just didn't want to review, so I kept playing and playing. In a way, that procrastination is also a form of anxiety — gaming becomes a way to escape; you know something important needs to get done, but you just don't want to do it, so you look for something to distract yourself with.

**5. Think back to a time when you planned to stop gaming to do something else, but didn't follow through. What happened?**

Nothing specific really happened at the time — it was mostly an internal struggle, and gaming just ended up winning that struggle.

**6. Do you usually play alone, play with others, or interact with other players outside the game? How do these situations affect your gaming experience?**

For online multiplayer games, I basically never play alone, but for single-player games, I play by myself often — GTA V and Red Dead Redemption, for example, both of which I've put a lot of hours into. As for interacting with other players outside the game — for a long time, meeting people online was literally the only reason I played. Once we met up in person after connecting online, we'd play together more frequently in-game, and then arrange to meet up again in real life.

**7. Do other people influence when you start, continue, or stop playing? Can you give a specific example?**

Yes. For online games, I basically only play when a friend — or someone I'm interested in — comes online; I rarely start a session on my own.

**8. Can you tell me about a time when gaming affected other things you had planned to do — like sleeping, studying, working, exercising, or spending time with others?**

All of them were affected, honestly. Academically, during the period when I was most addicted, my combined score across five classes didn't add up to more than 50. Once, the school organized a team-building ski trip, and the night before, I played ranked matches for a full day and night straight without sleeping at all — I forced myself to go the next day and felt awful the whole time. In terms of exercise, I basically did none during that period. For work, during an internship I was also going through a lot of anxiety, and I'd end up choosing to game instead of going in — I might only show up at the office twice a week. As for relationships with other people, during that period I basically couldn't function around people in real life at all — it felt like almost no connection with the outside world.

During that period, being so absorbed in gaming also led to some pretty impulsive decisions. For example, back when I had a dog at home, my family wouldn't let me play games, so when they went out, I'd have to walk the dog but also wanted to keep playing — I rushed through the walk, and when I got back I realized I didn't have my keys, and my computer was still on inside. Not wanting to just wait around, I ended up jumping from the rooftop over to my apartment's balcony to get back in — looking back, that was a pretty reckless thing to do.

**9. Have you ever tried to control or change your gaming time? What methods did you try, and how effective were they?**

I never really tried — I was basically in a state of total indulgence. I was aware it might be a problem, but I never actively tried to control or change it; it felt more like something that would resolve itself naturally, once I reached a certain point where I just didn't feel like playing anymore. For me, gaming addiction was completely tied to my mental state at the time — I'd previously been diagnosed with severe depression and anxiety, and once those improved, the urge to game basically disappeared too. During that period it wasn't just gaming — I also had a drinking problem, and I'd often drink and play at the same time.

**10. Have you ever gone through a period of intentionally cutting back or completely stopping playing a game? What prompted that decision, and what happened afterward?**

I never actively cut back — it was more that it just naturally faded away on its own. Playing less and less happened alongside my mental state and overall wellbeing getting better — the two were completely tied together with my emotional state.

## Additional Background

My senior year of high school was the period when I was gaming the hardest — while everyone else was studying, I was gaming, and my grades were terrible as a result. I did my senior year of high school in Canada. I think it was because my parents were so strict back home that once I was abroad with no one watching over me, I just let loose completely — it was almost like a kind of retaliatory binge on gaming.
