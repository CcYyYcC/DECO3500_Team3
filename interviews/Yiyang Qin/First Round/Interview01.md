# Interview 1

- **Topic:** Competitive Gaming, Social Play, Losing Streaks, and Self-Control

## English Transcript

**Interviewer:** What types of games do you usually play, how often do you play, and what do you enjoy most about them?

**Interviewee:** I mainly play competitive games, especially FPS games such as *Delta Force* and *Arena Breakout*. I usually play every one or two days. I do not necessarily play every day because sometimes I have other things to do, but if I had enough free time, I probably would play every day. The main thing I enjoy is the feeling of beating other people. I have tried other competitive genres before, but if I realise that I am not very good at them and keep getting beaten, I lose interest quite quickly. FPS games are different because I already have experience with them and usually perform better, so they feel more satisfying.

**Interviewer:** How has your experience with FPS games changed over time? Can you describe a time when you played for a particularly long period?

**Interviewee:** When *Delta Force* first came out, I already had experience with other FPS games, so I adapted quickly and felt that I was playing well. As everyone became more familiar with the mechanics, the skill gap became smaller and I started losing more often, so gradually I played less. However, there have also been times when losing actually made me play much longer. Once I kept losing badly and became frustrated because I had played FPS games for such a long time and felt that I should not be losing that badly. I kept thinking that I should be able to do better, so I continued playing and eventually stayed up all night. I probably played for more than ten hours.

**Interviewer:** Why would losing make you continue in an FPS game, while losing in another game might make you quit?

**Interviewee:** Because with FPS games I have already experienced success before. I know what it feels like to be good at them. If I suddenly perform badly, I feel unwilling to accept it and want to prove that I can still play well. With some other games, I never experienced that success in the first place, so if I keep losing, I just stop caring and quit.

**Interviewer:** Do you prefer playing alone or with friends, and how does playing with other people affect when you start or stop?

**Interviewee:** I prefer playing with friends because random teammates often have poor coordination, and some people can create a lot of pressure. Playing with people I know is more comfortable. Social influence is also one of the reasons I started playing games like *Delta Force* in the first place, because many people around me were playing. If my friends stop playing for the day, I usually stop as well and might just lie in bed and use my phone. Sometimes I still play alone if I really want to play and nobody else is available, but generally my friends have a strong influence on whether I continue.

**Interviewer:** Has gaming ever interfered with your work, sleep, or other plans? Have you tried to control it?

**Interviewee:** Sometimes if I get too absorbed and stay up late, I have less energy at work the next day. I do not think gaming has caused any extremely serious consequences in my life, but I know there are situations where I should not be playing. For example, recently I have been working on something important while not having a job. I knew I should focus on that instead of gaming, so I physically moved my computer to my sister's shop. I went there every day almost like going to work, and when I returned home at night, I could not easily play. That was probably the most effective way I have used to control myself. Normally I also try to stop around one or two in the morning, although occasionally I still stay up all night.

**Interviewer:** Do you ever deliberately quit games because you think they are taking too much time or money?

**Interviewee:** Not really. Usually I stop because the game becomes less interesting, or because the people around me stop playing it. I also do not spend heavily on games, so money has not really been a reason for me to quit.

**Interviewer:** Besides FPS games, are there other types of games you enjoy?

**Interviewee:** I also like survival and crafting games. I realised that part of the reason is similar to FPS games: if I feel that I am good at something, I enjoy it. For example, if I build something and think it looks better than what other people make, that gives me satisfaction. If I feel I am bad at something, I lose interest much more quickly.
