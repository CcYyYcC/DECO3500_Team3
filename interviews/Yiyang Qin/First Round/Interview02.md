# Interview 2

- **Topic:** Gacha Games, Narrative Engagement, FPS Competition, Habits, and Time Management

## English Transcript

**Interviewer:** What types of games do you usually play, and what attracts you to them?

**Interviewee:** I mainly play gacha games and FPS games, and I play almost every day. With gacha games, I think characters, progression, and collection are very important. Compared with something like *Devil May Cry*, where the depth comes more from action mechanics and combos, gacha games rely more on character appeal, development systems, and long-term progression. A lot of people start playing because they like particular characters.

**Interviewer:** Can you describe situations where you planned to stop playing but continued instead? What tends to keep you in the game?

**Interviewee:** That definitely happens, but the reason depends on the type of game. In a gacha game, I might be in the middle of a story chapter and want to finish the whole section before doing something else. I do not really like stopping halfway through a story because I prefer experiencing it continuously, almost like watching a long movie. If what I need to do is not urgent, I may delay eating or doing something else until I finish. In FPS games, it is more about the result of the match. If the last game was not satisfying or I lost, I might think, "I will just win one game and then stop."

**Interviewer:** Do you think your lifestyle affects how easy it is to postpone other things for games?

**Interviewee:** Yes. When I was studying overseas, I had relatively few classes and most assignments had long deadlines. That made it easier to think that something could be delayed. If there is no immediate consequence, it becomes much easier to continue playing.

**Interviewer:** Can you give an example of narrative content keeping you engaged for a long time?

**Interviewee:** A recent story chapter in *Wuthering Waves* was quite good, so I played through the whole thing in one sitting. The story was emotionally engaging enough that I wanted to know what happened. Even after finishing it, I kept thinking about it and searched online for explanations, hidden details, and analysis. I have similar experiences with visual novels. If I start one character route, I often want to finish the entire route in one sitting. I would describe that more as immersion than addiction.

**Interviewer:** Apart from stories, what keeps you returning to long-term gacha games?

**Interviewee:** Gacha games are also designed to create habits. You have daily activities, resources to collect, character progression, stamina systems, and things like that. It can start to feel a bit like clocking in for work. Even if you are not especially interested in playing on that particular day, there are resources available and you know that if you do not log in, you lose them.

**Interviewer:** Have you ever used tools or strategies to control gaming or phone use?

**Interviewee:** Yes. My computer sometimes gives me hourly reminders to rest. I have also used scheduled phone-locking tools that block certain apps at night and only allow things like QQ or music. They can help, but they are not perfect. Sometimes the app does not run correctly in the background, the restriction fails to activate, and then I keep using my phone. When that happens, I may stay up very late and either wake up late or feel exhausted the next day.

**Interviewer:** Why do you think those failures happen?

**Interviewee:** I think it is often about short-term habits rather than something deeper. For example, if I play with friends in China while living overseas, the time difference may mean I eat dinner very late. After eating, I do not immediately go to sleep, so I use my phone for a while. One unusual late night can shift my sleep schedule, and then the same pattern continues for several days.

**Interviewer:** Has gaming ever affected something important you were supposed to work on?

**Interviewee:** Yes. Right now I should probably be putting much more time into my current project. Sometimes I think that because I am already behind, I should remove all entertainment and spend all of that time working. But in reality, I cannot completely eliminate entertainment. I still play games, even when I know I should probably spend more time on the project.

**Interviewer:** How do you see the relationship between FPS games and gacha games in terms of what keeps players engaged?

**Interviewee:** They are quite different. FPS games rely more on competitiveness, uncertainty, skill, and the desire to recover from a loss. Gacha games are more about characters, stories, progression, and long-term habits. Daily tasks are especially important because they encourage players to return regularly, even when they are not actively thinking, "I really want to play this game today."
