# Interview 3

- **Topic:** Casual Gaming, Social Interaction, Leisure, and Views on Addiction

## English Transcript

**Interviewer:** Do you play games often? What kinds of games do you usually play?

**Interviewee:** Very rarely. If I do play, it is usually something interactive with other people, especially simple games on the Switch. I prefer party games, cooperative games, or simple competitive games such as Mario-related games. I do not usually play complicated single-player games.

**Interviewer:** What do you enjoy about games when you do play?

**Interviewee:** Mainly the interaction with friends or my boyfriend. The fun comes from competing, cooperating, teasing each other, or deliberately causing trouble for each other. The game itself is not necessarily the most important part. It is more about the interaction happening through the game.

**Interviewer:** Can you describe a recent gaming experience?

**Interviewee:** Once my boyfriend and I were at a bathhouse and had nothing to do, so we played some games there for several hours. We first played a cooperative game. He was much better than me, and I kept getting stuck, so eventually I became frustrated because he had to complete parts for me. At that point, I felt the game had lost its meaning because I was no longer really participating. We switched to *Overcooked*, and that became even more competitive. We argued quite a lot. There was another couple nearby playing the same game and they were also arguing loudly. I think people with very strong competitiveness may not be well suited to some cooperative or coordination-heavy games.

**Interviewer:** Why would you choose a video game instead of something like a board game or a role-playing activity?

**Interviewee:** Video games usually have a lower barrier to entry and are easier to start and stop. Something like a murder mystery game can take several hours and is difficult to pause halfway through. A simple video game can be stopped whenever something else comes up, and you can save your progress or just leave it.

**Interviewer:** Do you ever play games alone?

**Interviewee:** Sometimes, but usually games with a more personal or relaxing experience, such as *Forza Horizon* or *Animal Crossing*. With *Forza*, I mainly enjoyed earning money and buying new cars. In *Animal Crossing*, I liked dressing characters and making things look nice. Those games can provide experiences that I may not have access to in real life.

**Interviewer:** Does gaming ever interfere with your plans?

**Interviewee:** Generally no. I only choose to play when I already have a continuous period of free time. I do not suddenly decide to play when I am very busy or when I only have a short fragmented break. For me, games are mainly a way of passing time rather than something that overrides my schedule.

**Interviewer:** Are there other leisure activities that have interfered with your plans?

**Interviewee:** Drinking has affected my plans more than gaming. Sometimes I drink because I want to relax and stop thinking about work or stress. Once I am enjoying it, I may stop caring about whatever plan I originally had. It has also caused conflict with people close to me because I sometimes prefer drinking alone, while they worry about my safety. There have been times when I did not tell them because I knew they would disagree, which created even more conflict.

**Interviewer:** Why is gaming more social for you, while drinking can be something you prefer doing alone?

**Interviewee:** Gaming itself is not relaxing or attractive enough for me unless there is interaction with another person. Drinking is different because it directly changes my mental state and helps me relax. I have also had bad experiences where being drunk around other people affected them emotionally, so sometimes I prefer not to disturb anyone and also do not want anyone disturbing me.

**Interviewer:** What do you think about people who become highly engaged with games?

**Interviewee:** I can understand it. Different people have different ways of getting entertainment, relaxation, exploration, competition, or emotional value. Some people prefer staying at home and exploring virtual worlds. I do not think gaming itself should automatically be judged negatively.

**Interviewer:** How would you distinguish normal enjoyment from addiction?

**Interviewee:** I think it depends on the effect on real life. If someone is completely ignoring work, study, money, and daily responsibilities because they only want to play games, then I would consider that unhealthy and something that may need intervention. But if someone spends a lot of time gaming and it does not really damage their life, I do not think the amount of time alone is enough to judge them.

**Interviewer:** What about someone who does not particularly want to play today, but logs in every day because a long-term game requires daily tasks?

**Interviewee:** I can understand that too. If they have a long-term goal in the game, then doing some repetitive daily activity may simply be the cost of achieving that goal. It may not be enjoyable every single day, but if the final result gives them enjoyment and it does not significantly interfere with normal life, I do not necessarily think it is a serious problem.
